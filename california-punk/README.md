# California Punk

From the LA hardcore of the late 70s, through the skate-punk labels of the 80s and the East
Bay's 924 Gilman Street, to 1994, when The Offspring, Green Day and their peers took punk to
the top of the charts.

## History

### LA hardcore (1977–1985)
Punk reached Los Angeles in 1977. The **Germs** (with singer Darby Crash and guitarist Pat
Smear) and **X** led the first wave, documented in Penelope Spheeris' film *The Decline of
Western Civilization* (1981). In the South Bay beach towns, **Black Flag** made punk faster,
heavier and angrier: "hardcore". Guitarist Greg Ginn's label **SST Records** became one of the
most important in American underground music, releasing Hüsker Dü, Minutemen, Sonic Youth,
Saint Vitus and Soundgarden. Black Flag's first singer Keith Morris formed the **Circle Jerks**.

Alongside them, **[Descendents](bands/descendents.md)** (sharing drummer Bill Stevenson with
Black Flag) wrote short, fast songs about girls, coffee and nerdy frustration, the template for
all later pop-punk. In Orange County, **[Social Distortion](bands/social-distortion.md)**
started out as snotty suburban punk and later mixed in country, rockabilly and Johnny Cash.

### Labels and skate punk (1980s – early 1990s)
In 1980–81 Bad Religion guitarist Brett Gurewitz started **Epitaph Records** to release his own
band. **[Bad Religion](bands/bad-religion.md)**'s *Suffer* (1988) set the "melodic hardcore"
sound: fast drums, three-part "oozin' aahs" harmonies, and intelligent, wordy lyrics by Greg
Graffin, who also has a PhD in evolutionary biology. In 1990 NOFX's Fat Mike started **Fat Wreck
Chords**. **[NOFX](bands/nofx.md)**, **[Pennywise](bands/pennywise.md)** and dozens of others
built the "Epi-Fat" skate-punk sound, spread through skate videos and surf culture.

### The East Bay: 924 Gilman (1986 – 1990s)
In Berkeley, the volunteer-run, all-ages club **924 Gilman Street** opened in 1986 with strict
rules (no alcohol, no racism, no major labels). It and the **Lookout! Records** label grew a
younger, poppier scene. **Operation Ivy** (Tim Armstrong and Matt Freeman) mixed punk and ska
and split in 1989. Armstrong and Freeman went on to form **[Rancid](bands/rancid.md)**.
**[Green Day](bands/green-day.md)** released two albums on Lookout!.

### 1994: the explosion
After Nirvana opened the door for alternative rock, punk followed in one year:
- **February:** Green Day's *Dookie* (major label, Reprise)
- **April:** The Offspring's *Smash* (Epitaph), which became the best-selling independent-label album ever
- **Also 1994:** Bad Religion's *Stranger Than Fiction* and NOFX's *Punk in Drublic*

**[The Offspring](bands/the-offspring.md)** and Green Day were suddenly on MTV constantly. The
scene argued about "selling out". Gilman banned Green Day after they signed to a major. The
**Vans Warped Tour** started in 1995 and became the summer home of the scene for 25 years.

### Pop-punk takes over (late 1990s – 2000s)
San Diego's **[Blink-182](bands/blink-182.md)**, with *Enema of the State* (1999), took the
Descendents' formula to the mainstream. Green Day reinvented themselves with the rock opera
*American Idiot* (2004). The Offspring kept scoring hits for decades.
