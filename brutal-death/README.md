# Brutal Death, Slam & Deathcore

This one is organized by style rather than city: death metal at its most extreme and
physical, from New York's brutal death metal through Texas slam to the international
deathcore and goregrind of today.

## History

### Brutal death metal (late 1980s – 1990s)
[Suffocation](../east-coast/bands/suffocation.md) took death metal to its physical extreme:
very technical riffs, blast beats, deep guttural vocals, and slow, crushing breakdowns. New
York (Suffocation, Immolation, Internal Bleeding) became the home of "brutal death metal".
Internal Bleeding is often credited with emphasizing the groove-heavy "slam" section.
[Dying Fetus](../east-coast/bands/dying-fetus.md) in Maryland added grindcore and hardcore grooves.

### Slam (late 1990s – 2000s)
In Texas, **[Devourment](bands/devourment.md)**'s *Molesting the Decapitated* (1999) built whole
songs around the slam: ultra-low, slow, bouncing riffs with gurgling, "pig squeal"-style
vocals. Slam became its own international underground, with labels like Unique Leader and
Sevared and bands across Europe, Russia, Asia and Oceania, New Zealand's
**[Organectomy](bands/organectomy.md)** among them.

### Gore and grind (Europe)
Carcass' goregrind (UK, late 80s) inspired a European line of gory, horror-themed brutal death
metal, led by Belgium's **[Aborted](bands/aborted.md)**: precise, savage, and full of horror-movie
samples.

### Deathcore (2000s – now)
In the 2000s, hardcore kids picked up brutal death metal. Montreal's
[Despised Icon](../quebec/bands/despised-icon.md) and [Beneath the
Massacre](../quebec/bands/beneath-the-massacre.md), with Suicide Silence, Whitechapel, Job for a
Cowboy and Thy Art Is Murder (Australia), mixed blast beats and slams with hardcore breakdowns
and became "deathcore". In the 2010s it got more technical and extreme. England's
**[Infant Annihilator](bands/infant-annihilator.md)**, born as a YouTube studio project, became
a byword for absurd speed and brutality. Lorna Shore brought symphonic, blackened deathcore to
a huge audience in the 2020s.
