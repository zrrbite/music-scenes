# Gorguts

- **Base:** Sherbrooke
- **Formed:** 1989
- **Key member:** Luc Lemay (guitar, vocals, the only constant member)
- **Style:** Death metal → avant-garde / dissonant technical death metal

## History
Luc Lemay formed Gorguts in Sherbrooke in 1989. The band signed to Roadrunner Records and
released *Considered Dead* (1991), a strong if fairly conventional death metal debut, then the
more technical *The Erosion of Sanity* (1993). Death metal's commercial slump hit soon after,
and Roadrunner dropped them.

With a new lineup (Lemay, guitarist Steeve Hurdle, bassist Steve Cloutier, drummer Patrick Robert),
Lemay wrote *Obscura*. Inspired partly by modern classical music, it threw out normal riffing
for dissonance, slides and harmonics. It was finished well before release and spent years
looking for a label, finally coming out on Olympic Recordings in 1998. At first it was
divisive. Today it's considered one of the most important death metal records ever made.

*From Wisdom to Hate* (2001) followed with drummer Steve MacDonald. MacDonald died in 2002 and
the band broke up soon afterwards. Lemay stepped away from music for several years and worked
as a woodworker **(verify)**.

Lemay re-formed Gorguts in the late 2000s with Kevin Hufnagel and Colin Marston (both of the
New York band Dysrhythmia) and drummer John Longstreth (Origin). *Colored Sands* (2013, Season
of Mist), a concept album about Tibet, was widely praised as a worthy follow-up to *Obscura*.
The single-song EP *Pleiades' Dust* (2016) told the story of Baghdad's House of Wisdom. Former
guitarist Steeve Hurdle died in 2012.

## Sound
Gorguts started as a solid early-90s death metal band and turned into one of the genre's most
radical. *Obscura* threw out conventional riffing for dissonant chords, slides, harmonics and
disorienting structures. It baffled people when it came out and has influenced dissonant
death metal ever since (Ulcerate, Artificial Brain, Pyrrhon and others).
