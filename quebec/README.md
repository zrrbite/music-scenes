# Quebec Death Metal

wave, and the modern progressive / tech-death bands coming out of Montreal and elsewhere in the province.

## A short history of death metal, and where Quebec fits

### Origins (mid-1980s)
Death metal grew out of the most extreme end of thrash. Bands like Slayer, Kreator and
Celtic Frost pushed speed, distortion and dark themes, and a handful of bands went further.
Possessed (San Francisco) recorded a song called "Death Metal" on *Seven Churches* (1985),
which helped give the genre its name. Death, led by Chuck Schuldiner in Florida, released
*Scream Bloody Gore* in 1987, often called the first true death metal album. The defining
traits came together quickly: low-tuned tremolo riffing, blast beats, growled vocals, and
themes of gore, horror, death and the occult.

### The first wave (late 1980s – early 1990s)
The genre settled into regional scenes:
- **Florida:** Morbid Angel, Obituary, Deicide, Cannibal Corpse (who moved there from
  Buffalo), many of them recorded at Morrisound Studios in Tampa.
- **Sweden:** Entombed, Dismember and Grave, with the buzzsaw guitar tone of Stockholm's
  Sunlight Studio. Gothenburg later spun off melodic death metal (At the Gates, In Flames, Dark Tranquillity).
- **UK and the Netherlands:** Bolt Thrower, Carcass and Napalm Death (overlapping with
  grindcore), Pestilence and Asphyx.
- **New York:** Suffocation, whose dense, slam-heavy approach is the root of brutal death metal.

### The technical and progressive turn (early 1990s)
Almost as soon as the genre existed, musicians started stretching it. Death's *Human* (1991)
brought in fretless bassist Steve DiGiorgio and jazz-trained players. Atheist's *Unquestionable
Presence* (1991) and Cynic's *Focus* (1993) mixed in jazz fusion, and Pestilence's *Spheres*
(1993) went further still. That opened the door to the technical, progressive and avant-garde
death metal Quebec became known for.

### Quebec's contribution
Quebec already had a strong metal culture through **Voivod**, whose dissonant, sci-fi
progressive thrash was a direct model for thinking outside standard riff patterns.
In the early 90s two bands put the province on the death metal map:
- **Gorguts** went from solid first-wave death metal (*Considered Dead*, 1991) to *Obscura*
  (1998), which rewrote what death metal guitar could sound like and became the template for
  "dissonant death metal".
- **Cryptopsy** made *None So Vile* (1996), a reference point for brutal/technical death
  metal and extreme drumming.

Through the 2000s the Montreal scene produced a stream of technical bands: Martyr, Quo Vadis,
Neuraxis, Augury, Unexpect, and the deathcore pioneers Despised Icon and Beneath the Massacre.
From the 2010s, Beyond Creation, First Fragment and their peers made Montreal one of the world
capitals of technical/progressive death metal. Fretless bass, neoclassical guitar and
jazz-trained musicianship became something like a regional signature.

People usually point to a few reasons it happened here: a dense, French-speaking scene where
the same musicians rotate through several bands; strong music schools and conservatories
feeding trained players into extreme metal; and a long line of local examples (Voivod →
Gorguts → Cryptopsy → today) that made pushing boundaries feel normal.
