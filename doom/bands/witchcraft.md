# Witchcraft

- **Base:** Örebro, Sweden
- **Formed:** 2000
- **Key member:** Magnus Pelander (vocals, guitar, songwriter, the only constant)
- **Style:** Retro doom / occult heavy rock

## History
After his band [Norrsken](norrsken.md) ended, Magnus Pelander started Witchcraft partly as a tribute to his
heroes, Pentagram and Roky Erickson. The early records were recorded with deliberately vintage
gear and production, aiming for an authentic 1970s sound.

Lee Dorrian's Rise Above Records released the debut *Witchcraft* (2004), followed by *Firewood*
(2005) and *The Alchemist* (2007), which made them leaders of the Swedish retro rock revival.
After a lineup overhaul, *Legend* (2012, Nuclear Blast) was heavier and more modern, and
*Nucleus* (2016) was darker and more progressive. *Black Metal* (2020), despite its name, is
essentially a quiet, acoustic Pelander solo record.

### Pentagram plus Roky Erickson
Pelander didn't choose between his two heroes. He combined them: Pentagram's heavy doom riffs
with the eerie psychedelia of Roky Erickson and The 13th Floor Elevators. Both shaped how the
band played and how it recorded.

**The sound and recording (from Pentagram):** to get the dense, dry groove of early-70s
Pentagram, Pelander and the debut's producer **(verify name)** used no modern equipment.
- *Witchcraft* (2004) was recorded in a basement, with the band playing live in one room onto
  analog reel-to-reel tape. The bleed between instruments is part of the sound.
- Guitars were played through vintage Orange tube amps without boosts or modern distortion
  pedals, using vintage hollow-body guitars (a Gibson ES-series model is mentioned) **(verify)**.
  The thin, dusty fuzz comes from pushing old tube amps hard.
- No digital editing and no pitch correction.

**The mood (from Roky Erickson):**
- **Vocals:** instead of the shouted stoner-rock vocals common in the 90s, Pelander sings in a
  fragile, passionate style close to Erickson's, with lots of vintage spring reverb.
- **Lyrics:** old horror, isolation and the occult rather than standard rock themes, mirroring
  Erickson's preoccupations.
- **Songwriting:** acoustic folk passages, shifting tempos and hypnotic repeated melodies,
  influenced by Erickson's solo work.

That combination of two old blueprints, played on old gear, set the template for the 2000s
Swedish occult and retro rock movement.
