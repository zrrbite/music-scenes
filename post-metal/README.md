# Post-Metal

Slow-building, heavy, atmospheric music: metal's weight combined with post-rock's patience.
Songs are long, dynamics swing from near-silence to walls of sound, and the vocals are
often just one more texture.

## History

### Neurosis (1985–1990s)
Post-metal starts with **[Neurosis](bands/neurosis.md)**. They began as an Oakland hardcore
punk band, and by *Souls at Zero* (1992) and *Enemy of the Sun* (1993) they had become
something else: slow, tribal, apocalyptic heaviness with samples, keyboards and long songs.
*Through Silver in Blood* (1996) is the template. The Melvins' slowness and the industrial
textures of bands like Swans were both part of the mix.

### Isis and the classic form (late 1990s – 2000s)
**[Isis](bands/isis.md)**, led by Aaron Turner, took Neurosis' heaviness and added post-rock
structure in the style of Mogwai and Godspeed You! Black Emperor: gradual build-ups,
repetition, clean guitar passages, and crushing peaks. *Oceanic* (2002) and *Panopticon*
(2004) defined "post-metal" as a genre. Turner's label Hydra Head Records released much of the
movement, and the scene's sound became known as "thinking man's metal".

### The European wave (2000s – now)
Europe took post-metal and made it its own:
- **[Cult of Luna](bands/cult-of-luna.md)** from Umeå (Meshuggah's town) made it vast and
  cinematic, with two drummers and huge, slow crescendos.
- **[The Ocean](bands/the-ocean.md)**, a Berlin collective led by Robin Staps, built enormous
  concept albums about geology, the deep sea and the history of life.
- **[Amenra](bands/amenra.md)**, from Belgium, turned it into ritual: their "Mass" records and
  intense live shows sit somewhere between sludge, religious ceremony and catharsis.

In the US, instrumental bands like **[Russian Circles](bands/russian-circles.md)** and Pelican
dropped vocals entirely.
