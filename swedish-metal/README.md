# Swedish Metal

Sweden has produced more influential metal per capita than almost anywhere. These notes
cover three lines: the Stockholm death metal sound, Gothenburg melodic death metal, and the
progressive / extreme line that runs from Meshuggah to modern bands like Vildhjarta and
Humanity's Last Breath.

## History

### Stockholm: the buzzsaw sound (late 80s – early 90s)
In the late 80s, teenagers in Stockholm's suburbs picked up the tape-trading death metal
underground. **Nihilist** became **[Entombed](bands/entombed.md)**, and alongside Dismember,
Grave, Unleashed and Carnage they made a distinct "Swedish death metal" sound. The key was the
guitar tone: a Boss HM-2 pedal with every knob turned up, recorded by Tomas Skogsberg at
**Sunlight Studio**. *Left Hand Path* (1990) is the template, and the HM-2 "chainsaw" tone is
still copied by bands worldwide.

### Gothenburg: melody (1990s)
On the west coast, bands kept the aggression but added twin-guitar harmonies inspired by
Iron Maiden and Swedish folk melody. **[At the Gates](bands/at-the-gates.md)**, **[In
Flames](bands/in-flames.md)** and Dark Tranquillity, many recorded at Studio Fredman, created
the "Gothenburg sound". *Slaughter of the Soul* (1995) became the blueprint for much of
2000s American metalcore.

### Opeth and the progressive line (1990s – now)
**[Opeth](bands/opeth.md)** took death metal somewhere else entirely: long songs mixing growls
with acoustic folk passages and 70s prog-rock influences. Katatonia, often grouped with them,
moved from doom-death to melancholic rock along a similar path.

### Meshuggah and its descendants (1990s – now)
In Umeå in the far north, **[Meshuggah](bands/meshuggah.md)** developed something new:
polyrhythmic, low-tuned riffs that cycle against a steady 4/4 pulse, played on 7- and later
8-string guitars. Their palm-muted, staccato "djent" tone named a whole genre in the 2000s
and 2010s.

A newer Swedish generation took that idea toward something darker and more atmospheric.
**[Vildhjarta](bands/vildhjarta.md)** coined **"thall"** for their dissonant, eerie, heavily
syncopated sound, and drummer, guitarist and producer **Buster Odeholm** is a link between them
and **[Humanity's Last Breath](bands/humanitys-last-breath.md)**, who push the style toward
crushing, blackened deathcore. The same lineage now includes bands across Europe and North America.
One example is the French–Norwegian duo **[Mirar](../djent-thall/bands/mirar.md)**, who fuse thall with electronic
production and Baroque counterpoint and reportedly work with Odeholm on production.

Sweden's doom and retro rock bands (Candlemass, Witchcraft, Graveyard) are covered in the [Doom](../doom/README.md) section.
