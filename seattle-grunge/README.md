# Seattle Grunge

How a small, isolated Pacific Northwest scene of the mid-80s produced some of the biggest rock
bands of the 90s, and how nearly all of them trace back to a handful of shared musicians.

## History

### Isolation and influences (early 80s)
Seattle in the early 80s was a long way, geographically and culturally, from the music
industry in Los Angeles and New York. Local bands had no realistic shot at a major label,
which freed them to play whatever they wanted to a small crowd of friends. The sound that
came out of it was a mix of things that weren't supposed to go together: the sludge and
heaviness of Black Sabbath, the noise and attitude of punk and hardcore (Black Flag's slow,
heavy *My War* was a big one), 70s hard rock riffs, and the abrasive weirdness of local
pioneers the U-Men and the **[Melvins](bands/melvins.md)**.

### Deep Six, Green River and Sub Pop (1984–1988)
In 1986 the C/Z Records compilation *Deep Six* gathered six local bands (Green River,
Soundgarden, Melvins, Malfunkshun, Skin Yard, U-Men) and is often treated as the scene's
birth certificate. **[Green River](bands/green-river.md)**, formed in 1984, was the key band:
almost everyone important later on passed through it or was one step away from it.

Bruce Pavitt and Jonathan Poneman turned **Sub Pop** from a fanzine into a label and
marketed the scene aggressively, with a consistent look, photographer Charles Peterson's
blurry live shots, and the "Seattle sound" as a brand. Producer **Jack Endino** recorded
nearly everything at Reciprocal Recording, which gave the early records a shared rough,
heavy sound. "Grunge" became the word for it.

### The split that started everything (1987–1988)
Green River broke up over direction. Mark Arm and Steve Turner wanted to stay raw and punk.
They formed **[Mudhoney](bands/mudhoney.md)**, whose "Touch Me I'm Sick" (1988) became the
defining Sub Pop single. Stone Gossard and Jeff Ament wanted something bigger and more
classic-rock. They teamed up with Malfunkshun's flamboyant singer Andrew Wood to form
**[Mother Love Bone](bands/mother-love-bone.md)**, which signed to a major label and was tipped for stardom.

### Tragedy and Temple of the Dog (1990)
Andrew Wood died of a heroin overdose in March 1990, just before Mother Love Bone's debut
album came out. His roommate Chris Cornell of **[Soundgarden](bands/soundgarden.md)** wrote
songs in his memory and recorded them with Gossard, Ament, new guitarist Mike McCready and
Soundgarden drummer Matt Cameron as **[Temple of the Dog](bands/temple-of-the-dog.md)**. A singer
from San Diego who had come up to audition for Gossard and Ament's new band, Eddie Vedder,
sang on "Hunger Strike". That new band became **[Pearl Jam](bands/pearl-jam.md)**.

### The explosion (1991–1994)
In about a year the scene went worldwide. **[Nirvana](bands/nirvana.md)**'s *Nevermind*
(September 1991) knocked Michael Jackson off the top of the Billboard chart in January 1992,
and Pearl Jam's *Ten*, Soundgarden's *Badmotorfinger* and **[Alice in Chains](bands/alice-in-chains.md)**'
*Dirt* all became huge. Major labels flew in to sign anything from Seattle, flannel became a
fashion trend, and Cameron Crowe's film *Singles* (1992) used the scene as its backdrop.
Many of the musicians found the attention uncomfortable.

### Loss and aftermath (1994–now)
Kurt Cobain's death in April 1994 is usually seen as the end of grunge's peak. Addiction
haunted the scene. Layne Staley (Alice in Chains, **[Mad Season](bands/mad-season.md)**) died
in 2002, Mike Starr in 2011, Chris Cornell in 2017, and Mark Lanegan of
**[Screaming Trees](bands/screaming-trees.md)** in 2022. The family tree kept branching all the
same: Dave Grohl formed **[Foo Fighters](bands/foo-fighters.md)**, which became
the family tree's biggest 21st-century branch, Cornell fronted Audioslave, Matt Cameron joined Pearl
Jam, and Alice in Chains carried on with William DuVall. Pearl Jam and Mudhoney are both still
going decades later, and Mudhoney is still on Sub Pop.

Documentary to watch: *Hype!* (1996).
