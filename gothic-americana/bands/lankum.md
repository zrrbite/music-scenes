# Lankum

- **Base:** Dublin, Ireland
- **Formed:** 2000 as Lynched; renamed Lankum in 2016
- **Members:** Ian Lynch (uilleann pipes, concertina, vocals), Daragh Lynch (guitar, vocals), Radie Peat (vocals, harmonium, concertina), Cormac Mac Diarmada (fiddle, viola)
- **Style:** Irish traditional folk played as dark, droning, slow-building music

## History
Brothers Ian and Daragh Lynch came out of Dublin's punk and squat scene before turning to
traditional Irish music. As Lynched they released *Cold Old Fire* (2014), traditional and
original songs sung with raw Dublin voices. They renamed themselves Lankum in 2016, partly
because the old name evoked lynching. The new name comes from "False Lankum", a version of
an old murder ballad.

Lankum treat folk songs as material for something heavy: drones from harmonium and pipes,
very slow tempos, swelling noise, and long, dark arrangements closer to doom or drone than
to pub folk. *Between the Earth and Sky* (2017) and *The Livelong Day* (2019) built a big
following outside folk circles. *False Lankum* (2023), produced by John "Spud" Murphy, was
nominated for the Mercury Prize, and its version of "Go Dig My Grave" is overwhelming.

## Why it's in this section
Many of the murder ballads and hymns at the heart of Appalachian and gothic American music
came from Irish, Scottish and English ballads brought over by immigrants. Lankum go back to
those Old World sources and find the same darkness that 16 Horsepower and Nick Cave found in
the American versions. Their heaviness also makes them a natural crossover for [doom](../../doom/README.md)
and [post-metal](../../post-metal/README.md) listeners.
