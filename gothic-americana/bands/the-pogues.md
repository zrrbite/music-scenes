# The Pogues

- **Base:** London (Irish-diaspora band)
- **Active:** 1982–1996, reunited 2001–2014
- **Key members:** Shane MacGowan (vocals, d. 2023), Spider Stacy (tin whistle), Jem Finer (banjo), James Fearnley (accordion), Cait O'Riordan (bass, early)

## History
Shane MacGowan came from London's punk scene with Irish roots. The Pogues (originally Pogue
Mahone, Irish for "kiss my arse") played Irish traditional music with punk speed and attitude.
MacGowan's songs are full of drink, exile, violence, poverty and tenderness. *Red Roses for Me*
(1984), *Rum Sodomy & the Lash* (1985, produced by Elvis Costello) and *If I Should Fall from Grace
with God* (1988) are the classics. The last includes "Fairytale of New York" with Kirsty MacColl,
which has become a Christmas standard.

MacGowan was fired in 1991 for unreliability, and The Clash's Joe Strummer briefly replaced him on
tour. He also recorded a duet of "What a Wonderful World" with Nick Cave (1992). MacGowan died in 2023.

## Why it's in this section
The Pogues are the punk-era bridge between Irish ballad tradition and rock, the same tradition
[Lankum](lankum.md) draw on, and a cousin to Nick Cave's murder ballads. Through Joe Strummer and
the Clash they also connect to [California punk](../../california-punk/README.md), Rancid especially.
