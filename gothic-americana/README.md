# Gothic Americana & the Denver Sound

Dark, Old Testament-haunted folk and country: banjos, bandoneons, murder ballads, revival-tent
fervor, and the shadow of the American West. The center of these notes is David Eugene Edwards
(16 Horsepower, Wovenhand) and the Denver scene around him, plus the artists who shaped it.

## History

### Roots
The music draws on very old sources: Appalachian folk, Primitive Baptist and Pentecostal
hymns, murder ballads, and the Old Testament. In the late 70s and 80s, punk-era artists began
turning those sources dark and intense. **[The Gun Club](bands/the-gun-club.md)**'s *Fire of
Love* (1981) fused punk with Delta blues and voodoo imagery, and **[Nick Cave](bands/nick-cave-and-the-bad-seeds.md)**,
an Australian obsessed with the American South, made the murder ballad and biblical imagery
central to his work.

### Denver (late 1980s – 2000s)
Denver turned out to be an unlikely center for this sound. The **[Denver
Gentlemen](bands/the-denver-gentlemen.md)** included David Eugene Edwards and Slim Cessna, and
from that circle came **[Slim Cessna's Auto Club](bands/slim-cessnas-auto-club.md)** and
**[16 Horsepower](bands/16-horsepower.md)**. Edwards, the grandson of a Nazarene preacher,
sang with fierce religious intensity while playing banjo, bandoneon and guitar.

16 Horsepower signed to A&M and released *Sackcloth 'n' Ashes* (1996). They were never big in
the US but were hugely popular in France and the Benelux countries, helped by their two French
members. People started calling the style of Denver bands (including Munly, Tarantella and
others) the **"Denver Sound"**.

### Wovenhand and beyond (2001 – now)
Edwards began **[Wovenhand](bands/wovenhand.md)** as a solo project while 16 Horsepower was
still active. When 16 Horsepower ended in 2005, Wovenhand became his main band, and it grew
heavier and more tribal over the years. Its later records came out on labels linked to heavy
music (Deathwish, Sargent House), and it toured with metal and post-metal bands. Edwards'
solo album *Hyacinth* (2023) continued in that direction.

The wider family includes Johnny Cash's *American Recordings* (1994), Jay Munly, Those Poor
Bastards, and, on the heavier side, Chelsea Wolfe and Emma Ruth Rundle, both on Sargent House.

Related: [Doom](../doom/README.md). Wovenhand fans often end up at Neurosis and post-metal.
